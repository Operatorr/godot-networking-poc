# Sanctuary — Town Redesign Goal, Theme & Creative Direction

## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the creative goal, design reasoning, theme, lore, color palette, and atmosphere for the redesigned Sanctuary town.

The Sanctuary is not just a menu hub. It is a playable place. It is the first environment players enter after choosing to enter the world, and it must immediately communicate what Omega Realm is:

A dying world.
A corrupted faith.
A civilization that has already lost.
A multiplayer refuge where survivors gather not because they are safe, but because this is the last wall still standing.

The previous Sanctuary layout was too clean, too symmetrical, and too cheerful. It looked like a crossroads with a fountain and service buildings placed around it. That structure was easy to understand, but it did not feel like a real medieval town, and it did not fit the tone of Omega Realm.

The new Sanctuary must feel like an old, dense, miserable, walkable medieval city: tight alleys, crooked roads, blackened stone, ruined chapels, old graveyards, plague houses, taverns, vendors, banks, player gathering spots, and portals to places no sane person would enter willingly.

The town should feel closer to a grim medieval pilgrim city than a clean fantasy hub.

---

# 2. Design Goal

The goal is to redesign Sanctuary into a believable multiplayer hub city that supports gameplay while expressing the dark fantasy identity of Omega Realm.

Sanctuary should feel like:

- A small medieval walled city.
- A holy town that has decayed into desperation.
- A place built over older ruins and sealed catacombs.
- A dense settlement with many cramped streets and alleys.
- A player lobby where people naturally gather, trade, talk, and prepare.
- A safe zone that still feels unsafe.
- A town with history, grief, corruption, and religious dread.

The player should not spawn into a clean central plaza. They should arrive through a damaged gate into a refugee yard, surrounded by tents, ash, carts, guards, other players, warning boards, and signs of collapse.

From there, they should gradually discover the town:

- The crooked fountain square.
- The tavern row.
- The merchant bend.
- The cathedral.
- The guild backstreets.
- The mage quarter.
- The lower portal yard.
- The world gate.
- Graveyards, ruins, parks, plague pits, and abandoned homes.

The Sanctuary should be readable, but not symmetrical. Players should navigate by landmarks, not by perfect cardinal directions.

# 3. What We Are Changing

## Old Direction

The old town was structured like this:

- Fountain in the center.
- Cathedral to the north.
- Portal to the south.
- Shops to the east.
- Guilds to the west.
- Straight roads crossing in the middle.

This made the town easy to parse, but it created several problems:

1. It felt artificial.
2. It felt too clean.
3. It felt too bright and safe.
4. It did not feel like a real medieval town.
5. It did not have enough density.
6. It did not have enough mystery.
7. It made every service building feel like a menu button.
8. It lacked alleys, history, ruins, and lived-in spaces.
9. It did not support the Diablo-like tone of despair.
10. It did not make Sanctuary feel important.

## New Direction

The new town is structured as a dense, ruined, medieval sanctuary city.

Instead of a crossroad, it uses:

- Crooked roads.
- Uneven districts.
- Narrow alleys.
- Dense building clusters.
- Ruined filler buildings.
- Interior spaces.
- Defensive walls.
- Graveyards.
- Dead gardens.
- A cathedral district.
- A merchant bend.
- A tavern row.
- A lower portal ward.
- A refugee spawn yard.
- A cursed fountain plaza.

The new Sanctuary should feel like a place that grew over centuries, not something placed by a level designer in five minutes.

# 4. Core Fantasy

The Sanctuary is the last inhabited holy city on the edge of collapse.

It was once a pilgrimage city built around a sacred fountain, a cathedral, and a buried relic beneath the old stone. Pilgrims came here for healing, blessing, confession, trade, and protection.

Then the world began to die.

The outer villages burned.
The roads filled with corpses.
The gods fell silent.
The cathedral bells cracked.
The fountain darkened.
The dead were buried in layers beneath the streets.
The lower sanctum was sealed.
Then reopened.
Then sealed again.
Then turned into a portal yard.

Now Sanctuary survives as a contradiction:

It is a safe zone, but nobody feels safe there.
It is a holy city, but faith here is desperate and ugly.
It is a marketplace, but everyone is selling things to people who may die within the hour.
It is a home, but only for those who have nowhere else to go.

Players gather here because outside the walls is worse.

# 5. Lore Premise

Sanctuary was not originally built as a fortress. It began as a shrine town around the Fountain of Saint Orra, a spring said to have healed plague, madness, and wounds that ordinary medicine could not touch.

As the world darkened, the shrine became a cathedral. The cathedral became a pilgrimage city. The pilgrimage city became a fortress. The fortress became a prison for the living.

The city walls were raised after the first corruption wars. The ramparts were expanded after the second. After the third, nobody had the stone, labor, or faith to repair them properly.

The dead filled the graveyards, then the cloisters, then the plague pits, then the cellars.

Beneath the cathedral lies the original sanctum: an older structure of unknown origin. Some priests claim it predates the city. Some say it predates humanity. The Church built over it, named it holy, and spent centuries pretending it was not watching them from below.

The Arena Portal and World Gate now stand in the Lower Sanctum district, where old catacomb doors were forced open and ritual stones were dragged into place. The portals are necessary, but nobody trusts them. The Portal Warden keeps the rituals stable with iron, prayer, and old blood.

The Fountain still flows.

That is the problem.

# 6. Tone and Vibe

Sanctuary should feel:

- Miserable.
- Ancient.
- Crowded.
- Religious.
- Gothic.
- Oppressive.
- Damp.
- War-torn.
- Haunted.
- Desperate.
- Player-inhabited.
- Beautiful in a ruined way.

It should not feel:

- Cheerful.
- Clean.
- Symmetrical.
- Cozy.
- Fairytale-like.
- Empty.
- Overly spacious.
- Bright.
- Like a town from a casual farming game.
- Like a menu disguised as a map.

The ideal emotional reference is:

> “This is the last place with walls, and even the walls are dying.”

The town should have the social functionality of an MMO hub, but the mood of a doomed settlement in a Diablo-like world.

# 7. Visual Direction

Sanctuary is a 3/4 top-down oblique pixel-art town.

The visual design should emphasize:

- Heavy stone.
- Cramped silhouettes.
- Uneven rooftops or roofless enterable ground floors.
- Black walls.
- Grey ash.
- Muddy roads.
- Broken cobbles.
- Tarnished religious detail.
- Cold magical glow.
- Firelight in windows.
- Dead vegetation.
- Blood-brown stains.
- Ruined statues.
- Hanging lanterns.
- Skull reliquaries.
- Prayer strips.
- Rusted iron.

The city should not be evenly lit. Light should be rare and meaningful.

Good light sources:

- Tavern windows.
- Forge fire.
- Candles near shrines.
- Portal glow.
- Mage tower cracks.
- Fountain glow.
- Braziers near gates.
- Cathedral altar.

Bad light sources:

- Bright daylight.
- Clean colorful banners.
- Cheerful market stalls.
- Saturated grass.
- Friendly village windows everywhere.

Most corners should remain dark.

## Palette Rules

The town should be mostly dark and muted.

Recommended balance:

- 50% Abyss Black / Iron Slate / Dark Umber.
- 25% Ash Grey / worn stone / mud.
- 10% Blood Brown / Rust Red.
- 10% Sulfur Yellow firelight.
- 5% Void Violet / Soul Teal / Corruption Magenta accents.

The eldritch colors should be rare. They should feel powerful because they are not everywhere.

# 10. Layout Philosophy

The town should not be organized like a cross.

It should be organized like a medieval place that evolved over time.

The logic should be:

1. A sacred spring existed first.
2. A shrine was built around it.
3. A cathedral replaced the shrine.
4. Pilgrims and merchants built around the cathedral.
5. Walls were added when the world became dangerous.
6. Houses filled every useful gap.
7. Wars damaged the walls.
8. Plague filled the graveyards.
9. Guilds moved into old structures.
10. Portals were forced into the lower sanctum.
11. Refugees now crowd the gate.

This creates a believable settlement history.

The player should feel that the town was not built for convenience. It was built by fear, faith, trade, and survival.

# 11. Navigation Philosophy

The town must be readable without being symmetrical.

Players should navigate using landmarks:

- “Spawn is at the West Gate.”
- “The fountain is in the crooked square.”
- “The cathedral is uphill to the north.”
- “The tavern is on the warm street.”
- “The bank is near Merchant Bend.”
- “The blacksmith is near the barracks.”
- “The rogue trainer is past the gallows.”
- “The mage trainer is in the cracked tower.”
- “The portals are in the Lower Sanctum.”

The player should learn the town by memory, not by compass.

Good town navigation:

- Curved roads.
- Multiple routes.
- Distinct districts.
- Strong landmarks.
- Shortcuts.
- Alleys.
- Visual lighting cues.
- Repeated silhouettes.

Bad town navigation:

- One central crossroad.
- Perfect symmetry.
- Every shop evenly spaced.
- Huge empty plazas.
- Identical buildings.
- No filler structures.

# 14. Atmosphere Rules

## Rule 1: Safe does not mean comforting

Sanctuary is a safe zone mechanically, but visually it should still feel grim.

## Rule 2: Light must be earned

The town should be mostly dark. Warm light should come from specific places: tavern, forge, candles, shrine, braziers.

## Rule 3: The fountain is not cheerful

The fountain is sacred and useful, but something is wrong with it.

## Rule 4: The cathedral is not hopeful

The cathedral represents faith under pressure, not salvation.

## Rule 5: Markets are desperate

Vendors sell survival, not luxury.

## Rule 6: Every alley should imply history

A player should be able to look at a corner and imagine something bad happened there.

## Rule 7: Ruins are part of the town

Ruins should not be decoration only. They should define paths, block sightlines, and create mood.

# 18. Short Lore Blurb

Sanctuary was built around a holy fountain that once drew pilgrims from across the realm. When the wars came, the shrine became a cathedral. When the dead came, the cathedral became a fortress. When the world began to rot, the fortress became the last place people still dared to call a city.

Now its walls are cracked, its streets are packed with refugees, its priests mutter prayers to silent gods, and its markets sell weapons to the already doomed. The fountain still flows in the crooked square, glowing faintly beneath the ash.

No one knows whether it is still holy.

No one dares stop drinking from it.

---

# 19. Final Creative Target

When a player enters Sanctuary, they should think:

> “This place is safe… but only barely.”

When they walk through the town, they should feel:

> “People live here because they have no choice.”

When they reach the portals, they should understand:

> “Leaving might kill me. Staying might be worse.”

That is the Sanctuary.
